## 1. General teaser mechanics

1. Silence is a repeated tool, not a one-off. Silences get longer and the return of sound harder; the scare lives in the seconds before the hit, when nothing is confirmed wrong. [FROM-SNIPPET: tonalchaostrailers.com horror trailer music article; soundcy.com jump-scare article]
2. A pull-down of music/ambience for a beat right before a sting makes the sting land harder by contrast. Stings are built from reverse swell, short impact, sharp hit, fast tail. [FROM-SNIPPET: soundcy.com / umbrellacreators.com]
3. Horror trailer music has moved toward processed sound design (granular voices, reversed and pitch-shifted piano, warped music boxes) over orchestra. [FROM-SNIPPET: tonalchaostrailers.com]
4. Shots are held early and shortened act by act; cuts hit hardest on the music's hits; then the montage stops dead on the title. In setup, roughly 2-4 s per shot with sparse music. Trailers typically use 3 acts. [FROM-SNIPPET: filmeditingpro.com and flashboards.yaroflasher.com style guides; these are editing-tip sites, not research, so treat numbers as rules of thumb]
5. Classical Hollywood ASL about 8-11 s; contemporary action 2-4 s. [FROM-SNIPPET: vidpros.com / editing-guide pages]. Useful only as a ruler for contrast.

## 2. Per-reference principles

- P1. Retro-tech surface carries tone: deliberate low-res aesthetic plus melancholy rather than gore. [FROM-SNIPPET for aesthetic; GENERAL KNOWLEDGE that its trailers mix sparse UI/terminal beats with sudden grotesque cuts]
- P2. Withhold the monster; let interface text, static, and a lone figure imply a larger mystery. [GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]

**Stories Untold** (No Code; four episodes, one with a text adventure, one microfiche, one analog dials) [FROM-SNIPPET]

## 3. Craft references

**CCTV / DVR on-screen display conventions** [FROM-SNIPPET: cctvcamerapros, securitycameraking, reolink; GENERAL KNOWLEDGE for look]
- Camera name typically upper-left, date/time upper-right; formats and positions are configurable. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Analog/HD-coax systems: the DVR overlays the timestamp; IP systems: the camera does. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Hierarchical camera naming (zone, camera ID, view description). [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Look: monospaced or blocky sans, white or yellow, thin black outline, "REC" with a red dot, blinking colon or second counter. [GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]
- Timecode doubles as a clock: jumps, freezes, or skipping minutes can signal an anomaly without any extra shot. [INFERENCE]

**VHS / CRT artifacts in moderation** [FROM-SNIPPET: nobledesktop, graphixly, borisfx, pixflow, glitchest]
- Authentic VHS = tracking lines, chroma bleed, tape wobble, softness plus fine noise. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- "Subtlety reads as real tape; extremes read as filter." If the subject disappears, dial back. [FROM-SNIPPET, paraphrased]
- Chromatic aberration: small amounts suffice. Suggested noise 5-12% (tutorial rule of thumb). [FROM-SNIPPET]
- A pristine audio track under heavy VHS visuals breaks the illusion; add a touch of lo-fi sound. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Our rule: keep a constant low-level base (scanlines, grain, slight bloom), reserve heavy tracking/tear for one or two anomaly beats so it carries meaning. [INFERENCE]

**Forensic evidence photography and case-file typography** [FROM-SNIPPET: chromalabel, forensiccsi, forensicreader, carolina.com, fontvs, creativebloq]
- Evidence markers: yellow A-frame tents with bold black numbers (1-20), metric scale on the base. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Scale beside the subject on the same plane, camera parallel; close-up without scale first, then with scale. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- ABFO No. 2 L-shaped scale is the standard, with gray balance patch and crosshair circles. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Typewriter/monospace fonts (Courier-like) read as case files and bureaucratic paperwork; stamp-like distressed variants exist. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Design use: flat on-axis framing, harsh flash, clean label, then a detail that does not belong (a shadow, a second marker nobody placed). [INFERENCE]

**Sound design of analog devices** [FROM-SNIPPET: Sony support article, vttoth, patents; plus GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]
- CRT power-on: degauss thump/"ping", often relay-driven, then a rising static hiss. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Flyback whine about 15.6-15.7 kHz (NTSC/PAL line frequency), a thin constant tone that many adults barely hear. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Relay clicks, tape-deck mechanics (door, play, motor, tape handling), VHS load/eject are available as Foley libraries. [FROM-SNIPPET: library pages only, nothing on technique]
- Phone vibration: short buzz bursts on a hard surface, body-conducted, with a resonant table rattle. [GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]
- Layering idea: each device gets its own signature, so the audience learns the vocabulary and the silence afterwards has shape. [INFERENCE]

## 4. Principles to apply to NEXUS ECHO's 56 s teaser

Timings are proposals to test in animatic, not findings. Shot-length numbers follow the 2-4 s setup rule of thumb [FROM-SNIPPET] and my own scaling [INFERENCE].

| Beat | Time | Principle | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-5 s | One strong image plus a sound identity within 5 s; establish the surveillance grammar (OSD, timecode) immediately so later breaks mean something. Analog horror "starts normal". | Pre-title audio: low drone, faint CRT whine. No music hit yet. |
| Quiet world | 5-14 s | Long holds (about 3-4 s), almost no movement, everything neat and procedural. The audience should believe they understand the place. | Longest shots in the piece. Hold on empty space; let the timecode move. |
| Investigation | 14-24 s | Device interactions (scrubbing, zooming, evidence tag, case-file text). Investigation as the hook (The Operator, Her Story). | 2-3 s shots. Keep text legible; typography carries authority. |
| Anomaly | 24-30 s | One thing is wrong, and it is wrong in the data layer first (timecode jump, a frame that should be empty). Not a creature. | Pull the music bed down for a beat before the anomaly [FROM-SNIPPET technique]. |
| Escalation | 30-40 s | Shot length shrinks step by step (3 s, 2, 1.5, 1, 0.7...). Pulse tempo rises; sub-bass enters. Cut on hits. | Do not exceed about 10 cuts in this stretch; legibility collapses past that. |
| Fragment montage | 40-47 s | 0.3-0.6 s fragments, each a different evidence type (photo, tape, screen, label). Withhold the whole: partial figures, cropped faces. | One heavy VHS tear here only. Keep fragments non-repeating. |
| Hard cut to silence | ~47 s | Total audio drop, including room tone [FROM-SNIPPET: silence before the hit]. | Silence at least 1.0-1.5 s is a starting guess. |
| Final image | 47-52 s | One held frame, wide and still; the last image that haunts should be a place or an absence, with one small detail to discover. | Hold longer than feels comfortable (3-4 s). |
| Sting | ~50-53 s | One low tone or one short impact, not both plus a riser. Restraint. | Reverse swell only if the silence is short. |
| Title | 53-56 s | Title lands on or just after the sting; plain, short, no animation flourish; fade/cut to black. | Keep 2-3 s readable. No button gag. |

Cross-cutting rules:
1. Introduce each sound once, early, so it can be taken away later (CRT whine, relay, tape mechanic, phone buzz).
2. Make the timecode a character: continuous in the quiet world, jumping in the anomaly, frozen at the end [INFERENCE].
3. Keep artifacts constant but faint early; spend the big glitch on exactly one moment.
4. Show the investigator's tools more than the threat (Paranormal Activity "show the watcher", Her Story, The Operator).
5. Never explain. A teaser should leave the audience with a question and one exact image.

## 5. Opening hook comparison

**A: dark control room, six CRT monitors waking one by one on camera frames, crawling timecode**
- Why it might work:
  - Immediately states premise (many eyes, many places) and the surveillance grammar. [INFERENCE]
  - The "waking" cadence gives a built-in rhythm (six beats) and a natural place for the CRT power-on sound identity (degauss thump, whine). [FROM-SNIPPET for the sounds]
  - Reads as an institution/bureau (cf. Control's cold clinical space) [FROM-SNIPPET].
  - Easy to compose graphically; strong thumbnail frame.
- Risks: the most common surveillance-room cliche; can feel like a tutorial/UI demo; six sequential reveals can eat 5+ s with no mystery.
- Look for in the animatic:
  - Does the first monitor appear by about 1 s, and all six by about 4-5 s?
  - Do the monitors show different images, and does one carry a small wrongness that is not explained?
  - Is the whine/thump readable on laptop speakers?
  - Does the viewer know where to look at every moment, or is attention split across six panes?

**B: first-person dolly down an empty corridor with REC timecode**
- Why it might work:
  - First-person limits what is visible, a built-in withholding device (RE7 teasers) [FROM-SNIPPET].
  - A moving camera creates anticipation with zero cuts, so the hook is pure tension.
  - Sets up the ending cleanly if the final frame returns to the same corridor (echo/rhyme) [INFERENCE].
  - REC timecode makes "found footage as evidence" immediate (Blair Witch framing) [FROM-SNIPPET].
- Risks: handheld "walk down a hallway" is also a cliche; an empty corridor can read as nothing in the first 3 s; it shows the recorder's POV before we know who they are.
- Look for in the animatic:
  - Does something change subtly in the first 5 s (a door ajar, a light flicker) so it is not just geometry?
  - Is camera speed slow enough to dread yet fast enough not to bore?
  - Does the REC overlay and OSD design hold up on a dark frame?

Decision aid: if the ending uses the corridor (Ending A), Hook B makes it a bookend, while Hook A makes the corridor a surprise "different place" and gives the montage a visual home. Either way, test both with a cold viewer who has not read the script, and ask "what do you think this is?" after 5 s. [INFERENCE]

## 6. Ending comparison

**A: hard cut from rapid montage to a single silent frame of the recorder's empty corridor, then one low tone, then title**
- Why it might work:
  - Textbook contrast: acceleration, then a total stop; silence repeated and lengthened conditions dread [FROM-SNIPPET].
  - The last image is a place, not a monster, and rewards a second watch (what was in the corridor?).
  - One low tone is restrained: it works as the sting and as a bridge to the title.
  - Matches "stops dead on the title" convention [FROM-SNIPPET].
- Risks: silence that is too long can read as a playback error; "empty corridor" is a risk if nothing in it rewards looking; the tone can feel anticlimactic if the montage didn't earn it.
- Look for: silence length (try 0.8 s, 1.2 s, 1.8 s); whether viewers notice a detail in the corridor frame; whether the tone's pitch sits under the title without masking it.

**B: end on the silhouette emerging, cut straight to title**
- Why it might work:
  - Delivers a payoff image; stronger for a quick viewer or social feed.
  - Shorter; no dead time.
- Risks: shows the threat, which reduces the withholding built up (analog horror and The Ring favor non-legible imagery) [FROM-SNIPPET]; a silhouette is the most common horror final image; the "emerging" motion tends to be telegraphed.
- Look for: whether the silhouette is readable in under 0.5 s and still scary at 1080p and on mobile; whether the cut to title needs a sound hit, which would turn it into a jump-scare ending.

Decision aid: A fits the stated tone (investigation, found evidence). B is the safer fallback if cold viewers find A confusing. A hybrid worth animatic-testing: A, with the silhouette appearing for roughly 2-3 frames (barely perceptible) in the final corridor frame, or only after a second viewing. [INFERENCE]

## 7. What does NOT apply / clichés to avoid

- Audience-reaction format (Paranormal Activity): relevant idea is "show the watcher", not the format. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Blair Witch-style fake-reality marketing (claiming the footage is real): not applicable, and ethically murky for a product teaser. [FROM-SNIPPET for the campaign; INFERENCE for the judgment]
- Full-length trailer structure (2:30, three full acts, button gag): too long for a 56 s teaser. [FROM-SNIPPET]
- Constant glitch/VHS on every frame (reads as a filter; no payoff left), a loud horn on every cut, and 'NO SIGNAL'/red-eye monitors as the only scare. [FROM-SNIPPET for the first]
- Jump-scare face with scream sting as the ending.
- Text cards of "In a world..." or "Some places should stay hidden" style taglines; over-explaining via voiceover.
- Copying any specific shot order, music cue, or logo treatment from the references above.
